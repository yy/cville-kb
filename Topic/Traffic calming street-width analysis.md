The **traffic calming street-width analysis** is a civic data tool that identifies Charlottesville neighborhood street segments wide enough to be candidates for low-cost traffic calming. Built by [[Person/Josh Carp]] as part of his Cville Data project, it starts from the premise that wide streets encourage faster driving and that quick, low-cost interventions can help slow drivers down.

## What it shows

The analysis flags segments of neighborhood streets **over 36 feet wide** as potential candidates and points to three intervention types: defining parking lanes, repositioning parking lanes toward the center of the street, and narrowing the street with chicanes or curb extensions.

The tool carries its own caveat: the analysis may miss some streets that need calming and may include streets where calming would not be appropriate. It is an independent, resident-built resource, not an official city list.
