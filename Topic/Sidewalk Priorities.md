**Sidewalk Priorities** is the City of Charlottesville's program for identifying, ranking, and building new sidewalk segments. City staff from [[Organization/Neighborhood Development Services]] and Public Works jointly developed the priority list, drawing on the Bicycle and Pedestrian Master Plan, the Streets That Work design guidelines, and small area plans completed since 2015.

## Prioritization

In 2022 the city received a grant from Virginia's Office of Intermodal Planning and Investment to re-evaluate sidewalk priorities with updated data and consolidate the various plans and studies into a single implementation list. Staff scored candidate segments on five factors: connectivity, safety (crash data and vehicle volumes), suitability (Walk Score, proximity to schools and transit, slope), demand (pedestrian trip patterns), and feasibility (right-of-way, utility and stormwater conflicts, trees, paving schedules, funding prospects, and equity). The city reports that 94 percent of the proposed sidewalks fall in areas where one or more priority populations — non-white, low-income, youth, elderly, disabled, or limited-English-proficiency residents — currently live.

Projects are sorted into four tiers: **Committed** (already funded, potentially underway), **Tier 1** (shorter segments with fewer construction conflicts), **Tier 2** (longer segments or whole blocks with more cost uncertainty), and **Tier 3** (large projects needing outside coordination with UVA, Albemarle County, or VDOT and additional funding). The city stresses that the tiers reflect feasibility, not priority — staff advance projects from all tiers simultaneously.

## Funding and delivery

The published priority list carries an estimated cost of $4,524,700 across fiscal years 2025–2030: $733,000 (FY25), $639,800 (FY26), $901,100 (FY27), $753,600 (FY28), $1,017,100 (FY29), and $480,100 (FY30).

Design and construction sit with the **Capital Development Division** of Public Works, which handles the planning, design, and construction of the city's capital improvement projects, including bicycle and pedestrian improvements and multimodal roadway work. As of June 2026 the division's published staff directory lists Steven Hicks, Director of Public Works.

## Status

The public-facing Sidewalk Priorities page reflects the 2024 update: the public comment period closed April 30, 2024, with the final priority list scheduled for publication in June 2024. The page presents the FY2025–2030 program above and does not show revised construction timelines beyond it.
